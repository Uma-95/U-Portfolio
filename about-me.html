It all started with college fests and club events, pulling off fests, managing club juniors, and making sure things didn’t fall apart five minutes before go-time. Late nights, last-minute fixes, and “who forgot the mic?” moments, those were my bread and butter. I didn’t realize it back then, but all that behind-the-scenes chaos was my gateway into the world of Product.

What started as organizing college events turned into a full-on curiosity spiral: Why do some things just work? What makes people show up, stay engaged, and actually enjoy the experience?

🛣️

Fast forward to AlgoUniversity, and suddenly I was in the thick of it, planning national bootcamps like the Graph Programming Camp, managing 200+ students, cooking up Telegram engagement strategies, and deep-diving into market research. From feedback loops to cohort-building, I wasn’t just learning product thinking…I was living it.

Turns out that the same skills I used to run college events (minus the last-minute pizza orders) were the ones I needed to thrive in product: connecting dots, solving problems, and turning messy ideas into real experiences.

Now, instead of running around with crumpled to-do lists, I was doing it all with Notion, Airtable, and a lot more data. Same chaos, better tools.

📎

To me, curiosity has always been my compass, pulling toward questions like why things work, why users behave the way they do, and what makes some products feel effortlessly intuitive. That spark led straight into the world of Product, UX, and tech.

It’s still early days, but the mission is simple: build products and be a part of something that feel thoughtful, human, and genuinely useful.

There’s something powerful about digging into user behavior, obsessing over the little UX details, and leaning into the beautiful chaos of idea-to-execution.

It’s never about having all the answers, it’s about staying curious, listening with intention, and building things that make life just a little bit better.
