Account-Based Marketing (ABM) Strategy Exploration – Future AGI

Context

Future AGI was preparing to scale outreach to enterprise AI companies. My task as a Business Intern was to research how ABM agencies run campaigns and design a scrappy, startup-friendly version that could be applied at Future AGI.

Problem Statement

The team needed a scalable yet lightweight approach to account-based marketing, but lacked clear visibility into how traditional ABM agencies structure workflows vs. what a lean startup can realistically execute.

My Role

  • Conducted secondary research on ABM frameworks, campaign structures, and industry best practices.
  • Translated research into process flows that mapped how traditional agencies operate vs. how Future AGI could adapt these methods.
  • Created two flowcharts:
    1. General ABM Agency SOP (end-to-end process: account selection → personalization → outreach → feedback loop).
    2. Scrappy Startup Version (lightweight, resource-efficient ABM customized for Future AGI’s needs).

Outcome / Deliverables

  • Produced visual frameworks to guide leadership’s thinking on ABM execution.
  • Helped the business team align on trade-offs between resource-heavy agency-style campaigns vs. lean startup tactics.
  • Received direct feedback (“neat work”) from leadership, and work is being used to inform Future AGI’s early-stage GTM strategy.

Reflection

Working on ABM strategy taught me more than just marketing mechanics, it gave me product-relevant lessons about how growth decisions shape adoption:

  1. Bridging Strategy and Execution

    I realized that high-level GTM strategies often sound great in theory, but unless they’re broken down into concrete, repeatable workflows, teams can’t act on them. Translating a “playbook” into a flowchart forced me to think like a PM: how will this actually run day-to-day?

  2. Constraints Drive Creativity

    Traditional ABM agencies have endless resources like data vendors, creative teams, automated personalization. A startup doesn’t. Designing the “scrappy” version taught me to prioritize ruthlessly: What’s the smallest action that creates the biggest signal? This mindset is identical to prioritizing features in product roadmapping.

  3. User Insight > Volume

    ABM is not about blasting more emails; it’s about picking the right accounts, deeply understanding them, and tailoring outreach. That clicked for me as a product parallel: adoption and retention don’t come from “more features,” they come from the right features for the right users.

  4. Communicating with Stakeholders

    Presenting my frameworks to leadership taught me that neat visuals matter. A clean flowchart wasn’t just documentation; it was a way to align cross-functional teams quickly. This mirrors product management where you constantly need to sell your idea to engineers, designers, and leadership.

  5. Ownership Mindset

    Even though this wasn’t a core “product task,” I treated it like one: researching, designing, iterating, and presenting as if I owned the outcome. That reinforced for me that being “product-minded” is less about your job title and more about how you approach ambiguous problems.
