📝 PM Diaries

Some wins. Some flops. All lessons. This is where I document the things that shifted how I build, reflect, and navigate product work, from “what on earth!?” to “ohh that’s why” moments no course ever taught me.

swipe right for more lore 👉

Not Every User Interaction Deserves the Energy It Demands

🧠 What happened:

Some students approached me straight-up rude about not being shortlisted to the bootcamp. Spoiler: they never signed up. At first, I was mad. But then I realized I couldn’t match their energy. So I kept it calm, factual, and polite, even though it took every ounce of patience I had.

🎯 Takeaway:

In ops and product, not every frustration needs a reaction.

Sometimes, being polite isn’t about being nice it’s about being strategic.

Keep your cool, protect your energy, and never let someone else's bad day mess with your professionalism.

Always Talk to the User, Not Just the Stakeholder

🧠 What happened: During the onboarding phase of the bootcamp, I got the go-ahead from the placement POC and started onboarding colleges, all smooth on paper. Except... I hadn’t double-checked with students about their internal schedules. Turns out, many had exams… students messaged me after onboarding saying they had exams that week. The whole thing had to be postponed and recommunicated. It was chaos.

🎯 Takeaway:

Don’t assume top-down approval = ground-level readiness.

Always cross-check with the actual users, not just the stakeholders/gatekeepers.

Feedback Isn’t One-Size-Fits-All

🧠 What happened: After the camp, I reached out to students for feedback, but not everyone responded, and those who did didn’t always give usable info. That’s when I realized: you can’t copy-paste your ask across all users and expect results. Understanding the concept, Different segments, different language, different tone, and different approach helped.

🎯 Takeaway:

Feedback isn’t just about asking — it’s about how you ask.

Mix and match formats, tones, and timing based on the user you're talking to.

Personalization doesn’t mean it requires extra effort. It’s just a smart strategy.

Stick to the plan and always have a plan B in case of events involving huge numbers.

🧠 What happened: I was supposed to cross-check and ensure the students get their participation certificates according to their attendance, but in the end, I couldn’t handle the large crowd of students (at the offline event) on my own and ended up giving away wrong certificates and double to some students.

🎯 Takeaway:

Check your boxes beforehand while facing the users/students, in this case, directly. Always have a backup plan, assuming the worst-case scenario. In this case, I should have taken the help of my colleague while giving away the certificates and collectibles.

Communication Isn't Just Updates — It's Emotion Too

🧠 What happened: I thought I was communicating. Task updates? ✅. Timelines? ✅. But under the surface, I was overwhelmed, confused, and low-key burnt out and I didn’t say a word. That led to some real misalignments with my manager, where my silence got read as “all good,” when I was very much not all good.

🎯 Takeaway:

Communication isn’t just logistical, it’s emotional. Be honest about how you're doing, not just what you're doing. People can’t support what they don’t know exists.

Asking for Help Before You're Drowning is Smart and doesn’t make you look dumb. It’s ok to ask for help and figure out stuff.

🧠 What happened:

I used to overthink every tiny question before asking it on Slack. I'd rewrite the message five times, question if it was “worth their time,” and then just... not send it. I thought “I’ll figure it out” made me independent. In reality? I wasted hours, delayed progress, and isolated myself for no reason.

🎯 Takeaway:

Normalize asking questions before confusion becomes a roadblock.

Asking early can sometimes mean working smarter, not harder, given you do your research, and yet if you have confusion.

Don’t Wait to Be Told What You’re Allowed to Learn

🧠 What happened: In the beginning, I got comfortable doing what I was assigned: resume-making and communicating with the students about the JobOverflow platform. I assumed if I was meant to explore product strategy, growth, or user research, someone would hand it to me. Spoiler: they didn’t. And by the time I realized I could’ve just started exploring and asking, months had passed.

🎯 Takeaway: You don’t need permission to upskill. Own your curiosity. Upskill and choose growth both professionally and personally

Check if you’ve attached the documents. And for the love of clarity, track the email. 😑

🧠 What happened: I used to send emails and just… Hope for the best. No tracking, which led to awkward follow-ups on calls like, “Hey… did you check that email I sent?”, which didn’t leave a good impression on the other end. That’s when I realized: sending is just half the job. Knowing when, if, and how people respond? That’s the real management skill.

🎯 Takeaway:

Treat emails like mini-projects:

  • Double-check attachments
  • Track when you sent it
  • Set reminders to follow up
  • Don’t rely on memory; build a system to track these emails.

Always think 10 steps ahead, or at least don’t just do whatever is told. Ask yourself questions. Ask your colleagues, but don’t expect hand-holding. Just because your work is expected in a certain format, it’s totally ok to add your points and style of work, take feedback, and iterate.

🧠 What happened: I was given a task to create a master customer list from a DB, and I learnt the ICPs for FAGI, but the way I filtered and collected was not “impressive” or more like “maza nhi aya” to the person I was reporting. So what actually happened there was I made a basic sheet like literally the data to show and get feedback for the small part I did, but that person was expecting the final polished shiny sheet with datapoints and “insights” and I was sent back with the feedbacksaying “I didnt really understand the task in the first place, should have asked for more information and doubts when the task was given”

🎯 Takeaway: MENTION.NOT ONCE. BUT TWICE AND THRICE IF REQUIRED about what you are about to show and take feedback for + always make sure to show what you think also, along with what is expected, like if you are thinking 1 step ahead, showcase that in your work. Don’t wait for your manager to say something about it magically.NOPE.NOT CUTE.

If You Don’t Close Loops, People Spiral

🧠 What happened: While scripting the “Blue” episode, I realized: spiraling happens when the brain opens loops it can’t close. That’s not just an emotional insight , that’s a product principle. People spiral in apps, too: if a page doesn’t tell them what’s next, they drop off. If an action doesn’t resolve, they feel stuck.

🎯 Takeaway:

Emotional design = closing loops. Always show what’s next. End with clarity. End with calm.

Sometimes “Done” Means Letting It Go

🧠 What happened: I kept tweaking the freebie design and YouTube script, telling myself it “wasn’t ready.” But deep down, I was scared to let people see it. I realized perfectionism was just fear dressed in Canva fonts.

🎯 Takeaway:

Done > perfect. Feedback is more valuable than your anxiety’s opinion.
